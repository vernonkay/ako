# ako, vision and non-goals

ako exists because learning to run a manual lathe safely, on your own, is nearly
impossible to do well, and the people who used to teach it are disappearing. ako is a
patient 1:1 teacher that watches a real machine through a camera and a microphone and
coaches a complete beginner to competence, one guided project at a time, without ever
taking the human out of control.

## North star

**A 1:1 tutor that watches you at a real manual lathe and teaches you to run it safely
and well, from first start-up to skilled work, while you stay fully in control of the
machine.**

Three commitments we hold ourselves to, and measure features against:

- **Coach, never control.** ako observes, explains, verifies, and withholds approval. It
  has no connection to the machine and no way to stop or start it. The human is the only
  controller, and ako is designed around that fact rather than in spite of it.
- **The operator stays the thinker.** ako leans only on what it can perceive reliably.
  Everywhere else it hands the task back: measure this, work this out, tell me what you
  found. The learner does real reasoning, which is both more honest and how people
  actually learn a craft.
- **Start simple, and stay honest about what the camera can see.** We build on the
  perception that works today (a hand, a glove, a chuck key, the spindle turning, the
  sound of a cut) and treat the rest as teaching moments rather than pretending to a
  precision the sensors do not have.

## What ako is for

- A beginner's first safe hours on a lathe, with a teacher that never looks away.
- Guided projects that build real skill in order: machine control and safety, facing and
  turning, drilling, tapers and knurls, boring and reaming, threading, then precision
  multi-operation work.
- A second pair of eyes for a lone learner, a club, or a makerspace, filling the long
  gaps between the rare hours a human instructor is free.

## Non-goals, what ako is deliberately not

These are not "not yet". They are choices that keep the north star honest. A request that
pulls against one of them is usually declined, with a pointer back here.

- **Not a machine controller.** No actuation, no interlock wiring, no e-stop ako can
  trigger, ever. If a feature needs ako to stop or start the machine, it is the wrong
  feature. This is the deepest non-goal and the others protect it.
- **Not a replacement for a human instructor, a qualification, or a standard.** ako is a
  coach and a safety net, not an authority. Where a ticket, a code, or a supervisor is
  required, ako points at it rather than standing in for it.
- **Not a CNC or G-code tool.** ako is about the manual machine and the hand skills, the
  craft that a CNC deliberately hides. CNC is a different project.
- **Not an all-seeing oracle.** Where perception is unreliable, ako defers to the
  operator instead of bluffing. A confident wrong reading, telling someone a loose part
  is secure, is the single failure we design hardest against.
- **Not a surveillance or data-harvesting product.** A camera in a workshop is pointed at
  a person. ako should keep as much as possible on the machine, be explicit about
  anything recorded, and treat the learner's footage as theirs.
- **Not vapourware-by-scope.** We build the smallest honest thing that teaches safely,
  the certification gate and one guided project, before we build a curriculum. Breadth
  comes after the core is trustworthy.

## How this doc is used

When we weigh an idea, this is the reference for scope and direction. "Out of scope for
now" means maybe later, but not a fit today. "Pulls against the vision" means it works
against one of the non-goals above. Either way you get a real reply explaining which, and
why, see [`TRIAGE.md`](TRIAGE.md).

The vision can change, but slowly and in the open. If you think a non-goal is wrong, that
is a genuinely good [Discussion](https://github.com/vernonkay/ako/discussions).
